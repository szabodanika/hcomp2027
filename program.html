About the Program

HCOMP and CI each reviewed their own papers and talks. The two conferences ran independent program committees: work submitted to HCOMP was reviewed by the HCOMP committee, and work submitted to CI was reviewed by the CI committee. Each conference will publish its own proceedings in the ACM Digital Library. The HCOMP papers and talks are listed on this page, and the CI papers and talks are listed on the CI 2026 website.

The keynotes, workshops, posters and demos, and doctoral consortium were joint efforts between the two conferences. Those programs were planned by shared committees, and the accepted content below reflects submissions from both communities.

Titles will link to PDFs as camera-ready versions become available.

Industry • Joint Keynote

Eric Horvitz

Chief Scientific Officer

Microsoft

Monday, Sep 28, 2026

9:15 AM – 10:30 AM EDT

Talk title and abstract coming soon.

Eric Horvitz is Microsoft’s Chief Scientific Officer and Director Emeritus of Microsoft Research. He leads strategic initiatives at the intersection of science, technology, and society, with a focus on artificial intelligence, biosciences, and healthcare. A hallmark of his research is the development of principles for reasoning and action in time-critical, high-stakes contexts, where he pioneered methods to trade off the costs of delay and the quality of decisions.

His research has been recognized with the Allen Newell Prize and the Feigenbaum Prize for fundamental contributions to AI, as well as induction into the SIGCHI Academy for his work in human-AI interaction, including work on coordinating initiative, leveraging complementarity, and achieving common ground. He has played a significant role in formulating and guiding Microsoft’s approach to AI safety and in community-wide efforts, including co-founding of the Partnership on AI and the One Hundred Year Study on AI, and service on the National Security Commission on AI. He is a member of the National Academy of Engineering and the American Academy of Arts and Sciences, and is a Fellow of AAAI, ACM, and AAAS. He received his PhD and MD from Stanford University.

Academia • HCOMP Keynote

Diyi Yang

Assistant Professor of Computer Science

Stanford University

Tuesday, Sep 29, 2026

11:00 AM – 12:15 PM EDT

Human-AI Collaboration: Building Agents That Augment Work

AI agents are transforming how people work; however, building agents that augment workers requires knowing what they want from AI, how they collaborate with it, and whether that collaboration helps them. In this talk, we first audit the U.S. workforce to map where workers want automation, where they want augmentation, and where AI capabilities fall short, complemented by an analysis of nearly 250,000 real-world conversations.

We then introduce frameworks to measure collaboration directly by pairing workers with agents on real-world tasks to separate human and agent skill contributions, and by quantifying how much cognitive effort users offload through reconstructing how they would have worked without AI. Overall, this talk highlights how to understand and evaluate human-AI collaboration and how to design agents that augment human work.

Diyi Yang is an assistant professor in the Computer Science Department at Stanford University, also affiliated with the Stanford NLP Group, Stanford HCI Group and Stanford Human Centered AI Institute. Her research focuses on human-centered natural language processing and human-AI interaction.

She is a recipient of Microsoft Research Faculty Fellowship (2021), NSF CAREER Award (2022), an ONR Young Investigator Award (2023), and a Sloan Research Fellowship (2024). Her work has received multiple paper awards or nominations at top NLP and HCI conferences.

Government • CI Keynote

Laurie Allen

Creative Engagement Officer

Library of Congress

Wednesday, Sep 30, 2026

9:15 AM – 10:30 AM EDT

American Memory: A brief history of efforts to make Library of Congress materials broadly available since the beginning of the web

The Library of Congress has been leading and participating in efforts to make digital collections available online for more than 30 years. In 1995, Entertainment Weekly Magazine listed the Library of Congress site "American Memory" as one of the "Five Best Websites." Since then, Library of Congress staff have relied on a range of techniques and technologies to engage the American people with the information held on their behalf in the oldest federal cultural heritage institution in the United States (and the largest library in the world). Successive and overlapping strategies of curated websites, institutional collaboration, mass digitization, single-search-interfaces, crowdsourcing, collections-as-data, and machine learning through to contemporary uses of AI have challenged us to think across the disparate scales of human knowledge, cultural memory, and individual learning. As a way to explore these issues, the talk will describe a recent program from the Library of Congress to operate at the scale of local communities by co-creating digital experiences onsite inside public libraries, institutions whose approach to tackling the problems of scale offers an interesting complement to the challenges we face at the national level.

Laurie Allen is the Creative Engagement Officer in Researcher & Collections Services at the Library of Congress. She moved into this new position in 2026 after serving as the Chief of the Digital Innovation Division (LC Labs) in the Digital Strategy Directorate.

Before joining the Library of Congress in 2019, she spent nearly 20 years in academic libraries, leading efforts in scholarly communication, crowdsourcing, public data curation, and a variety of other projects where history, memory, art, and cultural heritage intersect with new technologies and new approaches to public engagement. Laurie lives in Philadelphia and holds a Bachelor's degree in Philosophy from Bard College, and a Master's of Library and Information Science from Simmons College.
